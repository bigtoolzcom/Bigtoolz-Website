#️⃣ What is a Hashtag Counter?

A Hashtag Counter is a simple tool that counts the number of hashtags in a piece of text. But it's more than just a counter our tool helps you analyze your hashtag strategy, detect duplicates, check platform limits, and optimize your reach. Every social media platform has different hashtag rules, and using the wrong count can hurt your post's performance.

Whether you're a content creator, social media manager, or business owner getting the hashtag count right is essential. Too few, and you miss discovery opportunities. Too many, and you look spammy and risk algorithmic penalties.

📊 Platform Hashtag Limits (2026)

📸 Instagram

Max: 30 hashtags per post. But more isn't better. Instagram's algorithm has been de-emphasizing hashtags since 2022. Studies show 8-15 well-chosen hashtags outperform 30 random ones. Mix 2-3 broad, 5-7 niche, and 3-5 specific-to-you hashtags.

𝕏 Twitter/X

Max: Unlimited (technically). But best practice is 1-2 hashtags per tweet. Tweets with 3+ hashtags see a 17% drop in engagement according to Twitter's own research. Use hashtags sparingly and only when truly relevant.

♪ TikTok

Max: 100 hashtags. But only the first 3-5 hashtags influence ranking. TikTok's algorithm prioritizes content, sounds, and engagement over hashtags. Use 3-5: 1 trending + 2-3 niche + 1 branded.

in LinkedIn

Optimal: 3-5 hashtags. LinkedIn allows more, but the platform's B2B focus means fewer, more relevant hashtags perform better. Stick to 3-5 that describe your post's topic, industry, or community.

▶ YouTube

Max: 15 hashtags in the description. Only the first 3 hashtags show above the video title. YouTube hashtags help with discovery but aren't as powerful as title and thumbnail optimization.

💡 Hashtag Best Practices

  • Relevance over reach. A hashtag with 10K posts that actually describes your content beats a 10M-post generic tag every time.
  • Rotate your sets. Using the exact same 30 hashtags on every Instagram post looks spammy. Rotate 2-3 sets.
  • Mix sizes. Combine 2-3 broad hashtags (100K-1M posts), 5-7 mid-tier (10K-100K), and 3-5 niche (under 10K).
  • Check for banned hashtags. Some hashtags are banned by Instagram (e.g., #adulting, #kissing, #desk). Search before using.
  • Put them in the caption, not comments. Since 2021, Instagram treats hashtags in comments as less relevant.
  • Skip spaces and punctuation. #MyTag works; #My Tag only counts #My.
  • Watch case sensitivity. Hashtags are case-insensitive but CamelCase (#MyTag vs #mytag) improves readability.
