👁 What is a Color Blind Test?

A Color Blind Test (also called a color vision test) measures how well you can distinguish different colors. The most common test is the Ishihara test a series of plates with colored dots where numbers or shapes are hidden inside. People with normal color vision see certain numbers; people with color vision deficiency (CVD) see different numbers or nothing at all.

Our free online color blind test uses canvas-generated plates in the Ishihara style. It's a quick, educational screening tool not a medical diagnosis. If you have concerns about your color vision, please see an eye doctor.

📈 How Common is Color Blindness?

  • Men: About 1 in 12 (8%) have some form of color vision deficiency.
  • Women: About 1 in 200 (0.5%) are affected.
  • Red-green: Accounts for 99% of all color blindness cases.
  • Blue-yellow: Extremely rare affects fewer than 1 in 10,000 people.
  • Total (monochromacy): Affects about 1 in 30,000 people worldwide.
  • Geography: Rates are similar worldwide, but color blindness is more common in populations of Northern European descent.

💡 What Causes Color Blindness?

  • Genetics: The most common cause. Inherited through the X chromosome that's why men are affected more than women.
  • Eye diseases: Glaucoma, macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy can cause acquired color vision loss.
  • Injury: Trauma to the eye or optic nerve can affect color perception.
  • Medications: Some drugs (like certain antibiotics or heart medications) can affect color vision.
  • Neurological conditions: Stroke, Alzheimer's, and multiple sclerosis can affect color processing.
  • Age: Natural aging reduces color sensitivity, especially for blues and violets.

💼 Living with Color Blindness

Color blindness is not a disability it's simply a different way of seeing the world. That said, some adaptations help:

  • Color-coded labels: Label items with text, not just colors (e.g., "Blue" instead of blue tape).
  • Special glasses: Brands like EnChroma help some people distinguish colors better.
  • Apps: Color identification apps can help name colors in real-time.
  • Contrast over color: Use high contrast for design instead of relying only on color.
  • Career planning: Some careers (pilots, electricians, police) may have color vision requirements check early.
