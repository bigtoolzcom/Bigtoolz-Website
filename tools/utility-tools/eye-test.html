👁 What is an Online Eye Test?

An online eye test is a quick screening tool that checks basic aspects of your vision visual acuity (how sharp you see), astigmatism (blurred/distorted vision), and color vision. It's useful for early detection of potential issues, tracking changes over time, or just satisfying curiosity.

Important: This is an educational screening, not a medical diagnosis. Online tests cannot replace a comprehensive eye exam by an ophthalmologist or optometrist. If you notice any vision problems, please see a professional.

📊 The 3 Vision Tests Explained

📖 Visual Acuity (Snellen Test)

The classic "20/20 vision" test. You read letters of progressively smaller sizes. If you can read smaller lines, your vision is sharper. Standard 20/20 vision means you can see at 20 feet what a person with normal vision sees at 20 feet.

🎯 Astigmatism Test

A grid of lines radiating from a center point. If all lines appear equal in darkness and sharpness, you likely have no significant astigmatism. If some lines look darker, blurrier, or doubled, you may have astigmatism (an irregular cornea shape).

🎨 Color Vision Test

Ishihara-style plates with numbers hidden in colored dots. This checks for red-green and blue-yellow color vision deficiency one of the most common inherited vision conditions (affecting ~8% of men).

💡 Signs You Should See an Eye Doctor

  • Blurry or double vision that doesn't clear up with blinking.
  • Frequent headaches or eye strain after reading/screen use.
  • Squinting to see clearly at distance or close-up.
  • Halos or glare around lights at night.
  • Floaters or flashes of light in your vision (emergency retinal issue).
  • Loss of peripheral vision or tunnel vision.
  • Difficulty distinguishing colors red/green especially.
  • Frequent prescription changes vision declining faster than expected.
  • Family history of glaucoma, macular degeneration, or cataracts.

🛡️ How to Protect Your Eyes

  • 20-20-20 rule: Every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  • Wear sunglasses with UV protection prevents cataracts and macular degeneration.
  • Eat eye-friendly foods: leafy greens, carrots, fish, citrus fruits (rich in lutein, zeaxanthin, omega-3).
  • Don't smoke: smoking doubles the risk of macular degeneration and cataracts.
  • Screen distance: Keep monitors an arm's length away; adjust brightness to match surroundings.
  • Regular exams: Even if you see fine, get checked every 1-2 years.
  • Manage diabetes: High blood sugar damages retinal blood vessels.
  • Stay hydrated: Dry eyes worsen blurry vision.
