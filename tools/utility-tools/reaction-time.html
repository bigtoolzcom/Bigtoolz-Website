⚡ What is a Reaction Time Test?

A Reaction Time Test measures how quickly you respond to a visual stimulus. In this test, you wait for the screen to change color and click as fast as possible. Your reaction time is measured in milliseconds (ms) thousandths of a second.

Reaction time is a fundamental measure of neural and muscular speed. It's used in gaming, sports science, driver safety research, and cognitive testing. Average human visual reaction time is around 250-300 milliseconds.

🧠 What Affects Reaction Time?

  • Sleep: Sleep deprivation can add 20-50 ms. Even one bad night affects reflexes.
  • Caffeine: 1-2 cups can improve reaction time by 10-20 ms. Too much worsens it.
  • Age: Fastest reaction times occur at ages 20-30. Decline is gradual (~5 ms per decade).
  • Time of day: Peak performance is usually early afternoon. Lowest is 3-5 AM.
  • Screen refresh rate: A 60 Hz screen adds ~16 ms vs. 144 Hz screen's ~7 ms.
  • Input device: Gaming mice add ~1-5 ms. Wireless adds 2-8 ms. Touchscreens add 30-80 ms.
  • Practice: Repeated testing improves anticipation, but not raw neural speed.
  • Distraction: Music, notifications, or multi-tasking reduce reflex speed.
  • Hydration: Even mild dehydration impairs cognitive and motor speed.

💡 Tips for a Better Score

  • Use a mouse instead of a touchscreen click response is 30-80 ms faster.
  • Wired connection beats wireless saves 2-8 ms of input lag.
  • Fullscreen the browser to eliminate visual distractions.
  • Look directly at the center of the color area don't track the edges.
  • Don't anticipate. Clicking early invalidates the round (false start).
  • Rest between rounds do 5 rounds max per session. Fatigue slows reflexes.
  • Test at the same time of day for consistent comparisons.
  • Be hydrated, rested, and not on caffeine crash.
