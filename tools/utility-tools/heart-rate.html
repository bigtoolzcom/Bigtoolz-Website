❤️ What is a Heart Rate Calculator?

A Heart Rate Calculator estimates your maximum heart rate (MHR) and breaks it into 5 training zones each targeting a different physiological benefit. Whether you're into running, cycling, HIIT, or just want to lose fat efficiently, training in the right heart rate zone makes every workout more effective.

Athletes have used heart rate zones for decades to avoid the two most common training mistakes: going too hard on easy days (leaving you fatigued) and too easy on hard days (not creating an adaptation). Training in the right zone = better results, less injury, faster progress.

📐 The Karvonen Formula

Our calculator uses the Karvonen formula the gold standard for accurate heart rate training:

Target HR = ((MHR − Resting HR) × Intensity%) + Resting HR

Where:
  MHR = Maximum Heart Rate
  Resting HR = Your resting heart rate (measured on waking)
  Intensity = Training intensity (50% – 100%)

Example: 30 years old, resting HR 70, target zone 70%
  MHR = 208 − 0.7 × 30 = 187
  Target HR = ((187 − 70) × 0.70) + 70 = 151.9 BPM

Why Karvonen is better than the simple percentage method

The simple method uses just MHR × Intensity% and ignores your resting heart rate. Two people with the same age but different fitness levels get the same targets but they shouldn't. Karvonen fixes this by factoring in Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) = MHR − Resting HR, which reflects actual fitness.

💡 Training by Goal

🔥 Fat Loss (60-70% Zone 2)

Train at Zone 2 for 45-60 minutes, 3-5 times per week. This zone uses the highest percentage of fat as fuel. It's sustainable, low-impact, and improves your aerobic base. The "conversational pace" rule: you should be able to talk in full sentences without gasping.

🏃 Endurance (70-80% Zone 3)

Train at Zone 3 for 30-60 minutes, 3-4 times per week. Improves heart stroke volume, capillary density, and mitochondrial function the "engine" behind endurance. You should be able to speak in short sentences.

⚡ Performance (80-95% Zones 4-5)

Mix Zone 4 tempo runs (20-40 minutes) with Zone 5 intervals (30 seconds to 5 minutes). This increases lactate threshold and VO2 max the two biggest factors in race performance. Only 2-3 hard sessions per week.

The 80/20 Rule

Elite endurance athletes spend 80% of training time in Zones 1-2 and only 20% in Zones 3-5. This "polarized training" minimizes injury and fatigue while maximizing adaptation. Most amateurs do the opposite (too much Zone 3), which leads to plateaus.
