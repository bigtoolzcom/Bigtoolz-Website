👂 What is a Hearing Test?

A hearing test measures how well you can hear sounds at different frequencies (pitches) and volumes. Professional tests use an audiogram a chart that maps your hearing sensitivity across frequencies. Our online test is a simplified screening that lets you check the frequency range of your hearing from low bass notes (20 Hz) to high-pitched tones (20,000 Hz).

This is educational only it cannot replace a professional audiogram. But it's a fun and useful way to track how your hearing changes over time.

💡 Signs You Should See an Audiologist

  • Frequent requests to repeat people always repeat themselves around you.
  • Difficulty following conversations in noisy places (restaurants, parties).
  • Turning up the TV/volume higher than others find comfortable.
  • Ringing in ears (tinnitus) or persistent buzzing.
  • Muffled sounds everything sounds like it's behind a wall.
  • Difficulty hearing high-pitched sounds (doorbells, birds, female voices).
  • One ear hears worse than the other.
  • Sudden hearing loss emergency, see a doctor immediately.

🛡️ Protecting Your Hearing

  • 60/60 rule: Keep headphone volume under 60% for max 60 minutes at a time.
  • Use earplugs at concerts, construction sites, or loud events.
  • Take listening breaks: Give your ears 5 minutes of silence every hour of headphone use.
  • Choose over-ear headphones over earbuds less direct pressure on the ear canal.
  • Use noise-cancelling headphones you can listen at lower volumes in noisy environments.
  • Avoid Q-tips: Never put anything in your ear canal you can damage the eardrum.
  • Keep ears dry: Prevent swimmer's ear and infections.
  • Manage health: Diabetes, high blood pressure, and smoking all affect hearing.
  • Get regular checkups: Especially if you work in loud environments.
