⭕ What is Tic Tac Toe?

Tic Tac Toe (also called Noughts and Crosses, XO, or Three in a Row) is a classic two-player game played on a 3×3 grid. Players take turns marking cells with their symbol X or O. The first player to get three in a row horizontally, vertically, or diagonally wins the game.

Despite its simple rules, Tic Tac Toe is a beautiful example of game theory and strategy. In fact, it's a "solved game" with perfect play from both sides, every game ends in a draw. That's why our Hard mode AI is truly unbeatable.

🧠 Winning Strategy Guide

Tic Tac Toe strategy is simple once you understand the priority of each square:

PrioritySquareWhy
1stCenter (5)Part of 4 winning lines maximum flexibility
2ndCorners (1, 3, 7, 9)Each corner is in 3 winning lines
3rdEdges (2, 4, 6, 8)Each edge is in only 2 winning lines weakest

Strategy Tips

  • Always take the center if available. It's mathematically the strongest opening move.
  • Create a fork set up two threats at once so your opponent can only block one.
  • Block immediately if your opponent has two in a row, block the third cell (or lose).
  • Corners beat edges. If center is taken, prioritize a corner over an edge.
  • Never play edge first. It's the weakest opening and gives your opponent an advantage.
  • Force a draw when losing. If you can't win, block every threat until the board fills.

🎯 Difficulty Levels Explained

😊 Easy

The AI plays random moves but blocks obvious wins. Perfect for beginners and children. You'll win most games at this level.

😐 Medium

The AI uses a basic strategy takes the center, blocks threats, and creates opportunities. It makes occasional mistakes, so smart players can still win.

😈 Hard (Unbeatable)

The AI uses the minimax algorithm it evaluates every possible future move and plays perfectly. At this level, you can only win if the AI makes a mistake (which it doesn't). Best case: a draw.

💡 Fun Facts

  • Ancient origins: Similar games have been played for over 3,000 years variants date back to ancient Egypt.
  • Solved since 1952: Mathematicians proved Tic Tac Toe always results in a draw with perfect play.
  • 8 winning lines: 3 rows + 3 columns + 2 diagonals = 8 possible winning combinations.
  • 255,168 possible games: But only 26,830 (10.5%) end in X winning, 26,830 in O winning, and the rest (78.9%) are draws.
  • Pop culture: Featured in the movie WarGames (1983) the AI realizes "the only winning move is not to play."
