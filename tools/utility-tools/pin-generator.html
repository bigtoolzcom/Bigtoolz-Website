#️⃣ What is a PIN Generator?

A PIN Generator creates random Personal Identification Numbers for phones, SIM cards, credit/debit cards, bank accounts, lockers, luggage, door locks, and any system that uses numeric or alphanumeric PINs for authentication. Instead of picking guessable numbers like your birthday or "1234," a good PIN generator gives you truly random, cryptographically secure codes that are impossible to predict.

Our tool uses the Web Crypto API the same cryptographic random number generator your browser uses for HTTPS encryption and password managers. Not a weak JavaScript Math.random() function. Every PIN is generated locally in your browser, never sent anywhere.

🎯 The 3 PIN Types Explained

🔢 Numeric

Standard 0-9 digits. Compatible with every system phones, SIM cards, ATM cards, door locks, bank accounts. The default choice. Length is limited to 4-12 digits.

🔤 Alphanumeric

Uses digits + uppercase + lowercase letters (0-9 A-Z a-z). Vastly more secure a 6-character alphanumeric PIN has 2.18 billion combinations vs 1 million for 6-digit numeric. Use this if the system allows it.

🎯 No Repeats

A numeric PIN where no digit appears twice (e.g., 4738 ✓ vs 4478 ✗). Reduces brute-force guessability further. Good balance between memorability and security.

🔐 PIN Security Guide

Recommended Length by Use Case

  • 4 digits: SIM cards, luggage, low-risk accounts (10,000 combos)
  • 6 digits: Phone locks, most bank cards, gym lockers (1 million combos)
  • 8 digits: Banking apps, encrypted storage, VPNs (100 million combos)
  • 12 digits: High-security systems, crypto wallets (1 trillion combos)

What NOT to Use as a PIN

  • 1234, 0000, 1111 most common PINs in the world
  • Birthdays (MMDD or DDMM) covers 1/3 of all guessed PINs
  • Your phone number's last 4 digits
  • Any sequential numbers (1234, 5678, 4321)
  • Repeated patterns (1212, 6969, 5555)
  • House or flat numbers

PIN Safety Tips

  • Never share your PIN with anyone including customer service. Banks never ask for it.
  • Cover the keypad when entering your PIN at ATMs or shops.
  • Use different PINs for different cards/accounts.
  • Change your PIN every 6-12 months or after any breach.
  • Store securely use a password manager, not a notebook in your wallet.
