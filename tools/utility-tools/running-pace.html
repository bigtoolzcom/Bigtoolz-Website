🏃 What is Running Pace?

Running pace is the time it takes to cover a specific distance usually expressed as minutes per kilometer (min/km) or minutes per mile (min/mi). It's the inverse of speed: a lower pace number means faster running. Example: 5:00 min/km pace = you run each kilometer in 5 minutes.

Our free running pace calculator helps you plan training runs and races. Enter any two of distance, time, or pace, and it instantly computes the third plus split times at every kilometer or mile, so you know exactly what your watch should show at each marker.

📐 Pace Formulas

Three simple relationships power all pace calculations:

Pace = Time ÷ Distance
Time = Pace × Distance
Distance = Time ÷ Pace

Example: 5K in 25:00 = 25 ÷ 5 = 5:00 min/km
Example: 5:00 pace × 10K = 50:00 finish time

Unit conversions you'll often need:

  • 1 mile = 1.609344 km or ≈ 1.61 km
  • 1 km = 0.621371 miles or ≈ 0.62 mi
  • Min/mi pace → min/km: divide by 1.609344
  • Min/km pace → min/mi: multiply by 1.609344

💡 Training Tips

  • 80/20 rule: 80% of your running should be at easy pace (2 min/km slower than race pace), 20% at hard pace.
  • Negative split your races run the second half slightly faster than the first. It's a proven race-day strategy.
  • Tempo runs: Run at "comfortably hard" pace about 20-30 sec/km slower than your 5K race pace.
  • Long runs: Should be 60-90 sec/km slower than your goal race pace to build endurance without excessive fatigue.
  • Interval training: 4-6 reps of 800m-1km at faster than 5K pace builds speed and VO2 max.
  • Race pace practice: Do at least 2-3 runs at your goal race pace before race day. Don't race at an untested pace.
  • Watch pace not distance: In hilly races, focus on consistent effort, not consistent pace.
