😴 What is the Sleep Cycle Calculator?

The Sleep Cycle Calculator helps you find the ideal bedtime or wake-up time by aligning with your body's natural 90-minute sleep cycles. Instead of waking up mid-cycle (which causes grogginess known as sleep inertia), you wake up at the end of a full cycle feeling refreshed and alert.

Sleep researchers have known for decades that humans sleep in roughly 90-minute cycles, progressing through light sleep → deep sleep → REM sleep and back. Waking at the end of a cycle naturally is easy. Waking mid-cycle (especially from deep sleep) feels awful. This calculator uses sleep cycle science to suggest the best times to set your alarm.

🎯 Understanding Sleep Cycles

Stage 1: Light Sleep (N1)

The transition from awake to sleep. Lasts 1-5 minutes. Easy to wake from you might not even feel like you slept.

Stage 2: Light Sleep (N2)

Deeper light sleep where heart rate and body temperature drop. Accounts for ~50% of total sleep. Memory consolidation begins here.

Stage 3: Deep Sleep (N3)

The restorative phase. Body repairs tissue, strengthens the immune system, and consolidates memories. Hardest stage to wake from this is where sleep inertia hits hardest. Most N3 sleep occurs in the first half of the night.

Stage 4: REM Sleep

Rapid Eye Movement sleep where most dreaming happens. Important for emotional regulation, creativity, and learning. REM periods get longer in the second half of the night.

💡 Tips for Better Sleep

  • Consistency beats duration. Same wake time daily even weekends anchors your body clock.
  • Light in the morning. 10-15 minutes of sunlight when you wake resets your circadian rhythm.
  • Cool, dark, quiet. The three pillars of a sleep-friendly bedroom.
  • No caffeine after 2 PM. Caffeine has a half-life of 5-6 hours a 4 PM coffee is still 50% active at 10 PM.
  • Limit alcohol. A nightcap helps you fall asleep but fragments REM sleep later.
  • Wind-down routine. 30-60 minutes of low-stimulation activity before bed.
  • If you can't sleep, get up. After 20 minutes awake, leave the bed. Lying awake trains your brain that bed = stress.
  • Consider a sleep tracker. Data from wearables can reveal patterns a calculator can't.
