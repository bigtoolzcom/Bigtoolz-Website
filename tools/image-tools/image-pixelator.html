🔲 What is Image Pixelation?

Image Pixelation (also called mosaic or block effect) replaces small groups of pixels with a single averaged color, creating a low-resolution or "8-bit" look. It's most commonly used to hide sensitive information like faces, license plates, credit cards, or addresses in photos. It can also be used creatively for retro or pixel-art aesthetics.

Unlike most online tools, BigToolz processes images 100% in your browser using the HTML5 Canvas API. Your images never uploaded to any server complete privacy, instant results.

🎯 When to Use Pixelation

🔒 Privacy & Censorship

Hide identities in news photos, blur license plates in car pictures, censor credit card numbers in screenshots, redact addresses in documents. Pixelation is more secure than blur for hiding sensitive text the original information is genuinely gone.

🎨 Creative Effect

Create retro 8-bit pixel art, add a "censored" look for social media, or design decorative backgrounds. Circle pixelation makes unique polka dot patterns.

📸 Artistic Aesthetic

Pixelate parts of a photo for a glitch-art look, create intentional low-res scenes, or use it as a transition effect in video edits.

📊 Recommended Block Sizes

  • 2-4 px: Light pixelation, still readable mostly aesthetic.
  • 5-8 px: Faces become unrecognizable, safe for privacy.
  • 9-15 px: Strong privacy hides text, numbers, IDs.
  • 16-30 px: Very heavy pixelation, only general shapes visible.
  • 31-60 px: Extreme abstracts the image to color blocks.

💡 Pixelate vs Blur: Which to Choose?

Use pixelation when you need to hide information completely pixelation is irreversible and cannot be un-pixelated by AI (unlike blur, which modern AI can sometimes partially recover). Use blur when you want a softer, more natural-looking effect for example, background blur or dreamy portraits. Check our Image Blur tool for the smooth effect.
