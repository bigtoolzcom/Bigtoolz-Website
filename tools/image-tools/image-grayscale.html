⚫ What is Grayscale Conversion?

Grayscale conversion removes all color information from an image, leaving only shades of gray from black to white. It's also called "black and white" or "monochrome." The result can feel timeless, elegant, and distraction-free often highlighting composition, contrast, and form better than color.

🎯 The 3 Grayscale Modes Explained

Luminosity (Recommended)

Weights colors by human perception greens become brighter, blues darker, reds medium. This matches how our eyes naturally see brightness, producing the most natural-looking black & white. Use this for 99% of photos.

Classic

Uses equal weights for red, green, and blue channels. Reds, greens, and blues all contribute the same amount to the final gray value. Sometimes produces a flatter result, but works well for technical/graphic content.

Desaturate

Sets the HSL saturation to 0 mathematically removing color. Similar result to Luminosity for most images, but produces subtly different tones. Good for experimenting.

💡 When to Use Grayscale

  • Portraits eliminates distracting colors, focuses on expression.
  • Architecture emphasizes lines, shapes, textures.
  • Street photography timeless, journalistic feel.
  • Product shots clean, minimal, premium look.
  • Documentaries adds historic/dramatic tone.
  • Design B&W backgrounds make colored elements pop.
  • Print cheaper to print, no color management.
