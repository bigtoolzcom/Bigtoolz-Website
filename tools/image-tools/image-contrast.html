◐ What is Image Contrast Adjustment?

Image Contrast controls the difference between the lightest and darkest parts of a photo. Higher contrast makes lights lighter and darks darker creating a punchier, more dramatic look. Lower contrast softens the image, giving a faded or vintage feel. Together with brightness and saturation, contrast is one of the three pillars of tonal editing.

🎯 Understanding the 3 Controls

◐ Contrast

The main control. 100% is original; below = flatter, above = punchier. Ranges from 0% (pure gray) to 200% (extremely harsh black & white separation). For most photos, 110-130% is the sweet spot.

☀️ Brightness

Adds or subtracts light from every pixel. Use it to compensate when higher contrast makes the image feel dark overall. Combines well with contrast tweaks.

🎨 Saturation

Controls color intensity. 0% = grayscale, 100% = original, 200% = super vivid. Higher contrast pairs well with slightly higher saturation for a "vibrant pop" look. Lower both for a moody feel.

✨ Quick Presets Explained

  • Original no change (100/100/100).
  • Punch Contrast 125%, Brightness 100%, Saturation 115% instant pop for social media.
  • Soft Contrast 85%, Brightness 105%, Saturation 95% dreamy, gentle look.
  • High Contrast 150%, Brightness 95%, Saturation 105% high-impact drama.
  • Dramatic Contrast 170%, Brightness 85%, Saturation 90% moody, cinematic feel.

💡 Best Practices

  • Contrast without brightness adjustment looks fake. When you increase contrast, shadows get darker add a touch of brightness to compensate.
  • Watch the blacks. Above 150% contrast, shadow details can "crush" (turn pure black) and become unrecoverable.
  • Watch the whites. Bright areas can "blow out" (turn pure white) at high contrast + high brightness. Balance the two.
  • Match your goal. Social media likes punchy (120-130%). Editorials prefer subtle (105-115%). Art projects can go extreme.
  • Use Punch as a baseline for social, then dial back if it looks overdone on a big screen.
