ℹ️ What is Image Metadata?

Image metadata is hidden information embedded inside a photo file. It's not visible when you look at the image, but any tool that reads it can see it. The most common format is EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format), originally created for digital cameras but now used by all modern devices.

Metadata can include camera make and model, shutter speed, aperture, ISO, focal length, date/time, GPS coordinates, orientation, software used, and much more. Phone cameras typically include GPS location, which is a serious privacy concern if you share photos online.

Unlike most online tools, BigToolz processes images 100% in your browser. Your images never uploaded to any server complete privacy, especially critical when inspecting metadata that may itself contain sensitive info.

📋 What Metadata Can You Find?

📷 Camera Info

Make (Apple, Samsung, Canon), Model, Lens Model, Focal Length, Aperture (f-number), Shutter Speed, ISO sensitivity, Metering mode, Flash status, White balance.

📍 GPS Location

Latitude, Longitude, Altitude, Timestamp. Most phone photos contain GPS data unless location services were off. Our tool provides a clickable Google Maps link when GPS data is found.

📅 Date & Time

DateTimeOriginal (when shot), DateTime (last modified), DateTimeDigitized (when digitized). Critical for organizing photo libraries but also tells others when you were somewhere.

🖥 Software & Editing

Software (e.g., "Adobe Photoshop 2024", "iPhone 15 Pro"), ProcessingSoftware, EditingHistory. Reveals what tools were used on the photo.

🎨 Image Properties

Dimensions, Color Space (sRGB, Adobe RGB), Bit Depth, Orientation, Resolution (DPI), Compression type.

✍️ Copyright & Credits

Artist, Copyright, ImageDescription, UserComment. Rare but useful for attribution.

🔒 Why Remove Metadata?

  • GPS privacy: Photos taken at home, work, or during travel reveal your location pattern.
  • Device fingerprinting: Camera serial numbers and exact model info can identify you.
  • Timestamps: Reveal when you were somewhere problematic for "I was working" situations.
  • Professional context: Client work should never leak your private software or editing workflow.
  • Job applications: Some employers check metadata on submitted portfolios.
  • Social media: Most platforms strip metadata, but direct messages and emails preserve it.
