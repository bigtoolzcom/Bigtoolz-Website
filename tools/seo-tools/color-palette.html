🎨 What is a Color Palette Generator?

A Color Palette Generator creates harmonious color combinations for your design projects websites, apps, brand identities, and print materials. Instead of guessing which colors work together, you get mathematically harmonized palettes based on color theory. Our tool also extracts palettes from images perfect for matching a brand to a photograph or pulling colors from a mood board.

Everything runs in your browser. Upload an image or pick a base color, choose a harmony mode, and generate unlimited palettes no signup, no limits, no data sent to servers.

🎯 The 5 Harmony Modes Explained

🎯 Complementary

Two colors from opposite sides of the color wheel. High contrast, high energy great for CTAs (call-to-action buttons) and hero sections. Example: blue + orange.

🎯 Analogous

Colors adjacent on the color wheel think red-orange-yellow. Creates a calm, unified feel. Perfect for cohesive designs and gradients. Low contrast, easy on the eyes.

🎯 Triadic

Three colors equally spaced on the wheel (like blue, red, yellow). Vivid and balanced. Great for playful designs and children's brands. Watch out can feel busy if not balanced with neutrals.

🎯 Tetradic

Four colors forming a rectangle on the color wheel. The richest palette type allows many combinations. Best for complex designs where variety matters.

🎯 Monochromatic

One hue with varying lightness and saturation. Elegant, minimal, and always looks professional. Great for premium brands, luxury products, and editorial designs.

📏 The 60-30-10 Rule

A classic design principle you should always apply when using any palette:

  • 60% Dominant color (usually a neutral background)
  • 30% Secondary color (sections, cards)
  • 10% Accent color (buttons, highlights, CTAs)

This balance keeps designs harmonious and prevents any single color from overwhelming the viewer.

💡 Color Theory Essentials

  • Warm colors (red, orange, yellow) energetic, attention-grabbing. Use for CTAs and important UI elements.
  • Cool colors (blue, green, purple) calm, trustworthy. Great for backgrounds, body content, and tech brands.
  • Neutrals (white, gray, black, beige) the "silent" foundation. Never underestimate them 90% of great designs use them well.
  • Contrast matters always ensure sufficient contrast between text and background for accessibility (WCAG 4.5:1 minimum).
  • Less is more 3-5 colors is plenty for most projects. Don't overload.
