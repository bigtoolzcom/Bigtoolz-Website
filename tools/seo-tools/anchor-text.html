🔗 What is Anchor Text?

Anchor text is the visible, clickable text of a hyperlink. In this example <a href="/seo">SEO tips</a> the anchor text is "SEO tips". It's one of the most important on-page and off-page SEO signals because it tells search engines what the linked page is about.

Anchor text matters in two directions:

  • Internal links anchor text helps Google understand your site's structure and page topics.
  • Backlinks anchor text from other sites is a strong ranking signal, but it can also trigger penalties if over-optimised.

💡 Healthy Distribution Targets

  • Branded: 40-50% brand mentions build trust
  • Naked URL: 20-25% copy-pasted links look organic
  • Generic: 10-15% "click here" from user-generated content
  • Partial match: 10-15% semantic variations
  • Exact match: 3-5% keep this low to avoid penalties
  • Image: 2-5% natural for some niches

Warning: If exact-match anchor text exceeds 10%, you're at risk of over-optimization penalties. Google's Penguin algorithm (now part of core) detects unnatural anchor patterns. Diversify.

⚖️ Follow vs Nofollow

Links have a rel attribute:

  • Follow (no rel, or rel="") passes PageRank. Default for internal links and trusted sites.
  • Nofollow (rel="nofollow") tells Google not to pass ranking signal. Used for sponsored, paid, or untrusted links.
  • UGC (rel="ugc") for user-generated content (comments, forums).
  • Sponsored (rel="sponsored") for paid/affiliate links.

A natural link profile is usually 80-90% follow and 10-20% nofollow. If 100% of links are follow, that itself can look suspicious.
