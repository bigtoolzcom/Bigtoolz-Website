📖 What is Readability?

Readability measures how easily a reader can understand a piece of text. It's determined by factors like average sentence length, syllable count per word, and word complexity. Readability scores help writers ensure their content matches their audience whether that's a 4th-grade student, a college graduate, or a technical professional.

📊 The 6 Readability Formulas

1. Flesch Reading Ease

Most widely used readability test. Scores 0-100. Higher is easier. Based on average sentence length and syllables per word. Formula: 206.835 − 1.015 × (words/sentences) − 84.6 × (syllables/words).

2. Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level

Converts Flesch to a US school grade. A score of 8 means an 8th grader can understand it. Formula: 0.39 × (words/sentences) + 11.8 × (syllables/words) − 15.59.

3. Gunning Fog Index

Estimates years of formal education needed. Focuses on complex words (3+ syllables). A score of 12 means a high school senior can understand it. Formula: 0.4 × [(words/sentences) + 100 × (complex words/words)].

4. SMOG Index

Simple Measure of Gobbledygook. Standard for healthcare materials. Requires 30 sentences for accuracy. Scores equal US grade level.

5. Coleman-Liau Index

Uses characters instead of syllables (more accurate for digital text). Formula: 0.0588 × L − 0.296 × S − 15.8 where L = letters per 100 words, S = sentences per 100 words.

6. Automated Readability Index (ARI)

Uses character count and word count. Output is a US grade level. Good for quick technical writing checks. Formula: 4.71 × (characters/words) + 0.5 × (words/sentences) − 21.43.

🎯 What's the Ideal Score?

  • Web content: Flesch 60-70 (Grade 7-9) most versatile target.
  • Consumer blogs: Flesch 70-80 (Grade 6-7).
  • Business/technical: Flesch 50-60 (Grade 10-12).
  • Academic/legal: Flesch 30-50 expected for specialized audiences.
  • Healthcare (SMOG): Target SMOG 6-8 for patient materials.

💡 Tips to Improve Readability

  • Shorten sentences aim for 15-20 words average. Break long sentences in two.
  • Use simpler words "use" instead of "utilize," "help" instead of "facilitate."
  • One idea per sentence avoid cramming multiple concepts.
  • Prefer active voice "The team wrote it" beats "It was written by the team."
  • Break up paragraphs 2-4 sentences maximum per paragraph on web.
  • Use bullet points lists are easier to scan than dense text.
  • Read aloud if it sounds awkward, rewrite it.
  • Target your audience don't over-simplify technical content or over-complicate consumer content.
