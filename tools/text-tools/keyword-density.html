📊 What is Keyword Density?

Keyword density is the percentage of times a specific keyword or phrase appears in a piece of text relative to the total number of words. It's calculated as: (keyword count ÷ total words) × 100. For example, if your primary keyword appears 8 times in a 1,000-word article, its density is 0.8%.

📐 Understanding N-Grams

1-Gram (Single Words)

The most basic analysis. Reveals which individual words dominate your content. Use to spot over-repeated words or see if your primary keyword appears enough times.

2-Gram (Two-Word Phrases / Bigrams)

Two consecutive words. Reveals natural phrase patterns like "keyword density," "content marketing," or "search engine." Great for discovering long-tail keywords already present in your text.

3-Gram (Three-Word Phrases / Trigrams)

Three consecutive words. Best for identifying your actual semantic themes. If your 3-grams match your target topic, your content is well-focused. If not, you may be drifting off-topic.

🎯 Ideal Keyword Density

  • 0.5-1.5%: Safe, natural, Google-friendly. Recommended for most content.
  • 1.5-2.5%: Slightly aggressive but still safe with strong semantic variety.
  • 2.5-3.5%: Risky starts looking like keyword stuffing.
  • 3.5%+: Dangerous likely to be penalized or not rank well.

Remember: Google doesn't use simple density algorithms anymore. Semantic relevance, natural language, and topical authority matter far more than any specific density number.

💡 Best Practices

  • Focus on topics, not just keywords cover everything related to your subject.
  • Use synonyms and related terms Google uses LSI keywords to understand context.
  • Prioritize readability if content sounds robotic, users bounce and rankings suffer.
  • Check density after writing don't force keywords into every sentence.
  • Use the primary keyword in H1, first paragraph, and meta description natural placement beats repetition.
  • Analyze competitor content see what density top-ranking pages use.
  • Target long-tail phrases 2-grams and 3-grams often capture user intent better than single keywords.
