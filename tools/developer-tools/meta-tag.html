🏷 What are Meta Tags?

Meta tags are HTML tags placed inside the <head> section of a webpage. They don't appear visually on the page itself, but they tell search engines, browsers, and social media platforms crucial information about the page its title, description, author, image for sharing, and more. Meta tags control how your page appears in Google search results and when shared on social media.

Well-written meta tags can dramatically improve your click-through rate (CTR) from search engines and social platforms, even without changing your rankings. They're one of the most fundamental and often overlooked parts of on-page SEO.

📏 Ideal Character Lengths

  • Title tag: 50-60 characters. Google shows up to 60 chars desktop, ~78 mobile.
  • Meta description: 150-160 characters. Google shows ~155 on desktop, ~120 on mobile.
  • OG title: Same as your title (60 chars).
  • OG description: 60-100 characters for cleaner social preview.
  • OG image: 1200 × 630 px (1.91:1 aspect ratio) Facebook/LinkedIn standard.
  • Twitter Card image: 1200 × 628 px for summary_large_image.

🎯 Essential Meta Tags Explained

Title Tag

The single most important on-page SEO element. Appears as the clickable headline in search results and the browser tab. Include your primary keyword near the front, and brand name at the end.

Meta Description

A short summary under the title in search results. Doesn't directly affect rankings but strongly impacts CTR. Write compelling, action-oriented copy that answers the user's question.

Open Graph (og:)

Facebook's protocol (also used by LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Slack, Pinterest). Controls previews when your page is shared. Critical for social media marketing.

Twitter Card

Twitter/X's equivalent of Open Graph. Twitter falls back to OG tags if Twitter tags are missing, but including both ensures the best preview.

Robots

Controls how search engines crawl and index the page. index, follow is the default allow indexing and follow links. Use noindex for pages you don't want in search results (thank-you pages, admin panels).

Canonical URL

Tells search engines the "official" URL for a page prevents duplicate content issues when the same content appears on multiple URLs (with tracking parameters, http vs https, etc.).
