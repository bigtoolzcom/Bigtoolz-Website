⚖️ What is BMI (Body Mass Index)?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a simple numerical measure calculated from your weight and height. It's widely used by doctors and health organizations as a screening tool to categorize whether a person is underweight, at a healthy weight, overweight, or obese. BMI was developed in the 1830s by Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet and is now the standard metric used by the WHO and CDC.

📐 BMI Formula

Metric Formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)

Example: 70 kg ÷ (1.75 m)² = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9 (normal weight)

Imperial Formula

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height² (in²)

Example: 703 × 154 lb ÷ (69 in)² = 108,262 ÷ 4,761 = 22.7 (normal weight)

📊 BMI Categories Explained

Underweight (BMI < 18.5)

Being underweight can indicate malnutrition, weakened immune system, or underlying health issues. It's associated with risks like anemia, osteoporosis, and fertility problems. Consult a doctor if you're significantly below the healthy range.

Normal Weight (BMI 18.5 – 24.9)

This range is associated with the lowest risk of weight-related health problems. Maintain your weight with a balanced diet, regular exercise, and adequate sleep.

Overweight (BMI 25 – 29.9)

Being overweight increases the risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, and certain cancers. Losing even 5-10% of body weight can significantly improve health markers.

Obese (BMI ≥ 30)

Obesity significantly raises the risk of serious health conditions including heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, sleep apnea, and certain cancers. Consult a healthcare provider for a personalized plan.

⚠️ BMI Limitations

  • Doesn't distinguish muscle from fat. Athletes with high muscle mass often show as "overweight" despite being lean.
  • Doesn't account for age. Older adults naturally have more body fat at the same BMI.
  • Doesn't account for gender. Women naturally have more body fat than men.
  • Doesn't show fat distribution. Belly fat (visceral) is more dangerous than fat on hips/thighs, but BMI can't tell the difference.
  • Not accurate for children. Children and teens use BMI-for-age percentile charts, not adult BMI.
  • Ethnic differences. Asian populations may have higher health risks at lower BMI values.

Use BMI as a general screening tool, not a diagnosis. For a complete picture, consider waist circumference, body fat percentage, and consult a healthcare professional.
