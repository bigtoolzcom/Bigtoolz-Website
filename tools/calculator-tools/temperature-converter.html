🌡 What is a Temperature Converter?

A Temperature Converter converts temperature values between different measurement scales. Three scales dominate daily life Celsius (most of the world), Fahrenheit (USA, few others), and Kelvin (science). Two more exist for specialized use Rankine (US engineering) and Réaumur (historical, some European dairies).

Unlike length or weight, temperature conversions aren't simple multiplication they involve offset adjustments because different scales have different zero points. Our converter handles all the formulas automatically and shows you exactly what calculation was used.

📐 Temperature Conversion Formulas

Each scale pair has its own formula. Here are the most common:

Celsius ↔ Fahrenheit

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

Example: 25°C = (25 × 9/5) + 32 = 77°F
Example: 100°F = (100 − 32) × 5/9 = 37.78°C

Celsius ↔ Kelvin

K = °C + 273.15
°C = K − 273.15

Example: 25°C = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K

Fahrenheit ↔ Kelvin

K = (°F − 32) × 5/9 + 273.15
°F = (K − 273.15) × 9/5 + 32

Rankine & Réaumur

°R = °F + 459.67 (= K × 9/5)
°Ré = °C × 4/5

💡 Temperature Facts

  • −40° is the only temperature where Celsius and Fahrenheit are equal! (−40°C = −40°F).
  • Kelvin is the SI unit for temperature and doesn't use the degree symbol just "K".
  • Absolute zero (−273.15°C) has never been reached. Scientists have gotten within a billionth of a degree.
  • Room temperature isn't standard it varies between 20-25°C (68-77°F) depending on country and context.
  • Human body temperature averages 37°C but fluctuates 0.5°C throughout the day (lower in morning, higher in evening).
  • Fahrenheit was designed so 0°F was the coldest temperature achievable in 1724 (ice + salt + ammonia), and 96°F was body temperature (later refined to 98.6°F).
  • Celsius was originally defined with 0°C as boiling and 100°C as freezing reversed from today!
  • Sun's core reaches 15 million °C hotter than the surface at just 5,500°C.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. Example: 25°C = (25 × 9/5) + 32 = 77°F. For F to C: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9. Example: 77°F = (77 − 32) × 5/9 = 25°C. Our converter handles all combinations automatically.

Kelvin starts at absolute zero (the coldest possible temperature). Celsius starts at water's freezing point. Conversion: K = °C + 273.15. So 0°C = 273.15 K, and 0 K = −273.15°C. Kelvin has no degree symbol it's just "K".

Normal body temperature is 37°C or 98.6°F. A fever is typically considered 38°C (100.4°F) or higher. Hypothermia starts below 35°C (95°F). Body temperature varies slightly throughout the day lower in the morning, higher in the evening.

Absolute zero = 0 Kelvin = −273.15°C = −459.67°F. It's the theoretical lowest possible temperature where particles have minimum motion. Practically unreachable the coldest lab temperature ever achieved is a tiny fraction of a degree above it.

Rankine is an absolute temperature scale used primarily in US engineering and thermodynamics. It uses Fahrenheit-sized degrees but starts at absolute zero. Conversion: °R = °F + 459.67. Mostly obsolete outside aerospace engineering.

−40°! That's the only temperature where both scales read the same value: −40°C = −40°F. It's an interesting mathematical result of the conversion formula.
