🚀 What is a Speed Converter?

A Speed Converter (also called a Velocity Converter) converts speed measurements between different units. It's essential for travel planning, sports, aviation, maritime navigation, motorsports, and any situation where different speed units meet. Whether you're reading a car spec in km/h and converting to mph, or checking wind speeds in knots a speed converter gives you instant, accurate results.

Our converter handles 12 speed units across multiple systems: metric (m/s, km/h, cm/s, mm/s), imperial (mph, ft/s, yd/s, mi/min, mi/s), and aviation/maritime (knots, Mach). All conversions happen 100% in your browser no upload, no signup.

💡 Speed Facts

  • Knots measure speed in nautical miles per hour used in aviation and shipping because they align with latitude degrees.
  • Mach number is named after Austrian physicist Ernst Mach it's the ratio of speed to speed of sound in the medium.
  • Sound travels faster in water (1,480 m/s) and steel (5,960 m/s) than in air (343 m/s).
  • Speed of light in vacuum is 299,792,458 m/s exact by definition.
  • 1 knot = 1.852 km/h exactly, based on the international nautical mile.
  • The world's fastest car (ThrustSSC) reached 1,227.985 km/h in 1997 breaking the sound barrier on land.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Multiply km/h by 0.621371 to get mph. Example: 100 km/h = 62.14 mph. Or divide mph by 0.621371 to get km/h: 62 mph = 99.78 km/h. Quick mental trick: mph ≈ km/h × 0.6.

1 knot = 1.852 km/h = 1.15078 mph = 0.514444 m/s. Knots are used in aviation and maritime navigation. Ships and planes measure speed in knots because 1 knot = 1 nautical mile per hour, which relates directly to degrees of latitude on the globe.

Mach is speed relative to the speed of sound. Mach 1 = 343 m/s = 1,235 km/h = 767 mph at sea level (15°C). Mach 2 = twice the speed of sound. It varies with altitude and temperature at 11 km altitude, Mach 1 is only 1,062 km/h.

Multiply m/s by 3.6 to get km/h. Example: 27.78 m/s = 100 km/h. Or divide km/h by 3.6 to get m/s. This conversion is exact because 1 hour = 3,600 seconds and 1 km = 1,000 m so (1000 m) / (3600 s) = 0.2777... m/s per km/h.

For a human: 1,227.985 km/h (Mach 1.02) by Felix Baumgartner in 2012 during a stratospheric skydive. For a manned vehicle: 39,937 km/h by Apollo 10 in 1969 during lunar return. For a man-made object: Voyager 1 at 62,140 km/h. Speed of light: 1,079,252,849 km/h the universal speed limit.

Commercial jets cruise at about 900 km/h (560 mph, Mach 0.85). Regional turboprops fly at 500-600 km/h. The retired Concorde flew at 2,179 km/h (Mach 2.04). Some newer jets (Airbus A350, Boeing 787) are slightly slower to save fuel.
