👶 What is a Pregnancy Calculator?

A Pregnancy Calculator (also called a Due Date Calculator) estimates when your baby is likely to be born, how many weeks pregnant you are right now, which trimester you're in, and upcoming milestones. It's the first tool most expectant mothers use after a positive pregnancy test.

Our calculator uses the standard medical formula Naegele's Rule which adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period. This is the same method doctors use, and it's the most widely accepted estimate for a full-term pregnancy.

📐 How Pregnancy Due Date is Calculated

There are two common methods. Both give the same result:

Method 1 (LMP-based):
Due Date = LMP + 280 days (or LMP + 9 months + 7 days)

Method 2 (Conception-based):
Due Date = Conception Date + 266 days

Example: LMP = Jan 1, 2026
Due date = Oct 8, 2026
Gestational age = 40 weeks (280 days)

Why 280 Days?

Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last period, not from conception. Ovulation typically happens ~14 days into the cycle, so gestational age starts ~2 weeks before conception actually occurs. This is why 40 weeks is used even though the baby is only ~38 weeks old at birth.

📅 The 3 Trimesters

First Trimester (Weeks 1-13)

The most critical period for development. The baby's brain, spinal cord, heart, and other organs begin forming. Common symptoms: morning sickness, fatigue, breast tenderness, food aversions. Miscarriage risk is highest during this trimester.

Second Trimester (Weeks 14-27)

Often called the "golden period." Morning sickness usually fades, energy returns, and the baby's movements begin (around week 18-20). The baby's sex can be determined by ultrasound. This is when most anatomical scans happen (18-22 weeks).

Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40)

The final stretch. The baby gains most of its weight, lungs mature, and position for birth. Common symptoms: back pain, shortness of breath, Braxton Hicks contractions, frequent urination. Full-term is considered 37-42 weeks.

🎯 Key Pregnancy Milestones

  • Week 4: Positive pregnancy test, missed period
  • Week 8: First ultrasound, heartbeat detectable
  • Week 12: End of highest miscarriage risk, NT scan
  • Week 20: Halfway mark, anatomy scan, movements felt
  • Week 24: Viability milestone (with medical care)
  • Week 28: Third trimester begins, kick counts start
  • Week 37: Early term begins
  • Week 40: Estimated due date

💡 Important Notes About Due Dates

  • Only ~4% of babies are born on their exact due date.
  • Most births occur within 2 weeks before or after the estimated date.
  • Ultrasound is more accurate than LMP dating, especially between weeks 8-13.
  • Irregular cycles make LMP dating less reliable use conception or ultrasound.
  • IVF pregnancies have a precise due date based on embryo transfer day.
  • Always consult your doctor for a medically accurate due date.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Using Naegele's rule: due date = Last Menstrual Period (LMP) + 280 days (40 weeks). This is the standard method used by doctors worldwide. If you know the conception date, add 266 days instead.

Only about 4% of babies are born on their exact due date. Most births occur within 2 weeks before or after. The due date is an estimate, not a guarantee think of it as a "due window" of 37-42 weeks.

LMP dating assumes pregnancy starts on the first day of your last period 2 weeks before conception. Conception dating starts from the actual fertilization date. LMP dating gives an older gestational age by ~2 weeks, which is why 40 weeks is standard even though the baby is ~38 weeks old at birth.

A full-term pregnancy is 40 weeks (280 days) from LMP, or 38 weeks from conception. Anything between 37-42 weeks is considered full-term. Babies born before 37 weeks are preterm; after 42 weeks are post-term.

First trimester: weeks 1-13 (organ formation). Second trimester: weeks 14-27 (growth & movement). Third trimester: weeks 28-40 (final development and birth). Each has different milestones and symptoms.

No twins share the same LMP and therefore the same estimated due date. However, twins are usually born earlier (average 36 weeks). Full-term for twins is considered 37-38 weeks, not 40.

For IVF, the due date is calculated from the embryo transfer day: Day 3 transfer = 263 days from transfer to due date; Day 5 transfer = 261 days. Our calculator works for natural pregnancies for IVF, consult your fertility clinic for a precise date.
