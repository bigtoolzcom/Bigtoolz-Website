💰 What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal AND on the interest accumulated from previous periods. In simple terms: you earn interest on your interest. This creates exponential growth small differences in rate or time lead to dramatically different results.

Albert Einstein reportedly called compound interest the "eighth wonder of the world" and for good reason. Over long periods, compound interest can multiply your money many times over, far surpassing what simple interest could achieve.

📐 The Compound Interest Formula

The standard formula for compound interest is:

A = P × (1 + r/n)n×t

Where:
  A = Final amount (principal + interest)
  P = Principal (initial investment)
  r = Annual interest rate (as decimal, e.g., 10% = 0.10)
  n = Number of times interest compounds per year
  t = Time in years

Compound Interest (CI) = A − P

Example: ₹1,00,000 at 10% for 10 years, compounded quarterly:

  • P = 1,00,000
  • r = 0.10
  • n = 4 (quarterly)
  • t = 10
  • A = 1,00,000 × (1 + 0.10/4)^(4×10) = ₹2,68,506
  • CI = ₹2,68,506 − ₹1,00,000 = ₹1,68,506

⚖️ Compounding Frequencies Explained

The more often interest compounds, the faster your money grows:

Yearly (1×/year)

Interest is added once at the end of each year. Lowest compound interest effect.

Half-Yearly (2×/year)

Interest added twice a year. Common for bank fixed deposits in some countries.

Quarterly (4×/year)

Interest added every 3 months. Common for many savings accounts and FDs in India.

Monthly (12×/year)

Interest added every month. Very common for loan EMIs and some investment products.

Daily (365×/year)

Interest added every day. Highest compounding effect used by some savings accounts and crypto.

Continuous Compounding

Theoretical maximum compounds infinitely often. Formula: A = P × e^(rt). Some financial products use this.

💡 Key Insights

  • Rule of 72: Divide 72 by your annual rate to estimate doubling time. At 8%, money doubles every 9 years.
  • Time is king: ₹10,000 at 10% for 30 years grows to ₹1,74,494 but for 40 years, ₹4,52,593. Extra 10 years = 2.6x more.
  • Rate matters more than you think: 2% higher rate over 30 years can nearly double the final amount.
  • Compounding works against you on loans: Credit cards at 36% annual grow 4.5x in 5 years if unpaid.
  • Frequency has diminishing returns: Yearly → Monthly is a big jump; Monthly → Daily is small.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. This "interest on interest" makes your money grow exponentially over time the longer you invest, the faster it compounds.

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t), where P = principal, r = annual rate (as decimal), n = compounding frequency per year, t = time in years, and A = final amount. Compound interest = A − P.

More frequent compounding = more interest. Example: ₹1,00,000 at 10% for 10 years yields ₹2,59,374 yearly, ₹2,65,330 quarterly, and ₹2,70,705 monthly. Daily compounding gives the highest amount but the difference between frequencies shrinks as they get larger.

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal flat growth. Compound interest is calculated on principal + accumulated interest exponential growth. Over long periods, compound interest can be many times greater than simple interest. Our tool shows both side-by-side for comparison.

The Rule of 72 estimates how long it takes to double your money: 72 ÷ annual return rate. Example: at 8% annual return, money doubles in ~9 years (72 ÷ 8). At 12%, in ~6 years. It's a quick mental math shortcut.

Quarterly is a balance between simplicity and benefit. It's frequent enough to give meaningful compounding growth, but easier to administer than monthly. Many savings accounts and fixed deposits use quarterly compounding. Some high-yield savings accounts use monthly or daily.
