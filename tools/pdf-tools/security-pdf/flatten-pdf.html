🔒 What is Flatten PDF?

Flatten PDF merges all interactive elements form fields, annotations, signatures into a single, static layer. After flattening, the document looks exactly the same, but nothing can be edited or clicked anymore. It's the digital equivalent of printing a form and signing it with a pen[citation:4][citation:13].

Unlike most online tools, BigToolz processes everything 100% in your browser using pdf-lib. Your PDF never uploaded to any server complete privacy. Flattening is lossless all your entered data stays visible, just frozen in place.

🛡 When to Flatten a PDF

1. Before Sharing Filled Forms

If you filled out a W-9, tax form, contract, or application and need to email it, flatten it first. Otherwise the recipient can accidentally (or intentionally) modify your answers. Flattening locks the data exactly as you entered it[citation:6][citation:13].

2. Before Archiving

Long-term archives often require PDF/A compliance, which prohibits interactive form fields. Flattening ensures your documents pass archival validation and render identically in 10 years[citation:13].

3. When Printing Fails

Some printers and print services render form widgets incorrectly missing fields, wrong alignment, or blank areas. Flattening converts everything to static content, guaranteeing prints match your screen[citation:5].

4. To Fix Compatibility Issues

Old XFA forms (Adobe-proprietary, deprecated) don't render in Chrome, Firefox, or mobile viewers. Flattening converts them to standard static PDFs that open everywhere[citation:3][citation:13].

⚠️ Important: Digital Signatures

If your PDF has a cryptographic digital signature (the kind verifiable with a certificate chain), do not flatten it. Flattening removes the signature widget, which invalidates the cryptographic proof. The PDF will still show your name as a static image, but it's no longer verifiably signed[citation:6].
