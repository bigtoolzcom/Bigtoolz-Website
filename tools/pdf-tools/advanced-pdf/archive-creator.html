📦 What is PDF/A?

PDF/A is an ISO-standardized version of PDF (ISO 19005) designed specifically for long-term digital preservation. While regular PDFs may lose fonts, colors, or accessibility over decades due to software changes, PDF/A files are guaranteed to display identically for 50+ years. It's the format used by libraries, government archives, courts, universities, and any organization that needs documents to remain readable forever.

PDF/A achieves this by restricting features that depend on external resources: all fonts must be embedded, encryption is forbidden, external content is disallowed, and audio/video is stripped. This makes PDF/A files self-contained and self-describing.

📚 The 3 PDF/A Versions Explained

📘 PDF/A-1b (ISO 19005-1)

The strictest and oldest version, based on PDF 1.4. Limits include:

  • No transparency support
  • No file attachments
  • No encryption or password protection
  • No JavaScript, no audio/video
  • All fonts must be embedded

Best for: Maximum compatibility, government archives, legal documents with the strictest requirements.

📗 PDF/A-2b (ISO 19005-2)

The most popular version, based on PDF 1.7. Adds:

  • Transparency support
  • JPEG2000 image compression
  • Optional content groups (layers)
  • Embedded PDF/A files

Best for: Most modern archival needs the recommended choice for organizations.

📙 PDF/A-3b (ISO 19005-3)

Based on PDF/A-2b but allows any file format embedded XML, spreadsheets, source images, video clips. Ideal for regulatory filings where the PDF is the container and other files are the payload.

Best for: ZUGFeRD invoices, e-invoicing, regulatory submissions, technical documentation packages.

💡 Best Practices for Archival PDFs

  • Use PDF/A-2b unless you have a specific reason for 1b or 3b.
  • Fill in metadata title, author, subject help future readers identify the document.
  • Use descriptive keywords 5-10 comma-separated terms.
  • Keep the original PDF archive is for preservation, original for editing.
  • Verify with veraPDF for certified compliance, use veraPDF (free open-source validator) or Adobe Acrobat Pro's Preflight tool.
  • Store in multiple locations archival format doesn't replace backups.
