⊞ What is PDF N-up Layout?

N-up layout places multiple pages of a PDF onto a single sheet of paper. For example, "2-up" puts 2 original pages on 1 sheet, "4-up" puts 4 pages, and so on. It's the standard technique for printing handouts, saving paper, creating drafts, and preparing booklets.

📊 Choosing the Right N-up Layout

2-up (2 pages per sheet)

Best for reading handouts, lecture notes, and reports. Text is still readable at full page size. Common for textbook chapters printed for study.

4-up (4 pages per sheet)

Best for drafts, editing, and paper-saving. Text is smaller but still readable. Ideal for proofreading and markup.

6-up (6 pages per sheet)

Best for reference materials and slide handouts. Text is small suitable for presentations, not dense text.

9-up (9 pages per sheet)

Best for thumbnail previews and contact sheets. Text is tiny mostly for visual reference.

16-up (16 pages per sheet)

Best for contact sheets, visual overviews, and mini-booklets. Each page is the size of a thumbnail.

🎨 Page Order Explained

Row-major (L→R)

Pages fill left-to-right, then top-to-bottom. This is the standard reading order for English, Spanish, French, and most Western languages.

Column-major (T→B)

Pages fill top-to-bottom, then left-to-right. Common in East Asian reading orders (traditional Chinese, Japanese vertical).

💡 Common Use Cases

  • Handouts 2-up or 4-up for course materials.
  • Drafts 4-up to save paper while reviewing.
  • Presentations 6-up or 9-up for slide handouts.
  • Contact sheets 16-up for photographers and designers.
  • Booklet printing 2-up landscape for saddle-stitch.
  • Legal documents 2-up for double-sided reading.
