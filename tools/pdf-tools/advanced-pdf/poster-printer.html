🖨 What is PDF Poster Printing?

PDF Poster Printing (also called "tiling" or "rasterbating") enlarges a single PDF page across multiple sheets of paper, which you then assemble like a puzzle into a large poster. It's the standard way to make big prints without a plotter just a regular home or office printer and some tape or glue.

BigToolz runs 100% in your browser using PDF.js to render the source page and pdf-lib to generate the tiled output. Your PDF never uploaded to any server complete privacy, works offline.

📐 Choosing the Right Grid Size

2 × 2 Grid = 4 sheets

Approximately A2 poster size. Great for medium wall posters, classroom visuals, or small banners. Fastest to assemble.

3 × 3 Grid = 9 sheets

Approximately A1 poster size. The most popular choice a good balance between size and effort. Ideal for presentation posters, event signage, and family photos.

4 × 4 Grid = 16 sheets

Approximately A0 poster size. Large wall art or exhibition posters. Requires careful alignment but creates dramatic impact.

5 × 5 or 6 × 6 Grid = 25 or 36 sheets

Billboard-scale output. Use for stage backdrops, conference banners, or oversized photo walls. Expect to spend an hour or more assembling.

📏 Why Overlap Matters

When you print tiles and align them edge-to-edge, tiny printer inaccuracies (1-2mm shifts, uneven paper feed) create visible seams. An overlap of 5-10mm adds a repeating margin to each tile you trim, align, and glue without gaps. Most professional poster prints use 10mm overlap.

💡 Assembly Tips

  • Print on card stock for durability thicker paper hides seams better.
  • Trim with a rotary cutter scissors give ragged edges that show.
  • Lay out all tiles first to check the overall image before gluing.
  • Use double-sided tape or spray adhesive no wrinkles from wet glue.
  • Work on a large flat surface floor or large table.
  • Enable cut marks in this tool to see exactly where to trim.
  • Mount on foam board for a professional, rigid finish.
