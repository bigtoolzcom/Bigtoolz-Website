🖼 What is a PDF Background Adder?

A PDF background adder places a color, image, or gradient behind the existing content of your PDF pages. The original text and images stay fully visible the background is a new layer underneath. It's perfect for changing paper color, adding brand imagery, creating a "dark mode" reading experience, or giving your PDF a professional look.

Unlike most online tools, BigToolz runs 100% in your browser using pdf-lib. Your PDFs never uploaded to any server complete privacy, instant results, no file size limits.

🎨 The 3 Background Types

🎨 Solid Color

The simplest and most common. Choose any color (or preset), apply to selected pages. Great for:

  • Cream/beige reduces eye strain for reading.
  • Tinted colors matches your brand palette.
  • Dark navy/black "dark mode" PDF experience.
  • White clean up PDFs that have gray/colored backgrounds.

🖼 Image Background

Upload any image (JPG, PNG, WebP) and set it as the background. Perfect for:

  • Company letterheads logo and branding on every page.
  • Watermark-style imagery subtle brand presence.
  • Decorative patterns backgrounds for invitations, certificates.
  • Photo books creative backgrounds per page.

Fit modes: Cover (fill entire page, may crop), Contain (fit inside, may show gaps), Stretch (fill exactly, may distort), Center (original size, centered), Tile (repeat pattern).

🌈 Gradient

Smooth color transitions between two colors. Options:

  • Top → Bottom most common, subtle vertical fade.
  • Left → Right horizontal gradient.
  • Diagonal corner-to-corner, dynamic.

Popular pairs: cream → peach, mint → teal, navy → purple.

💡 Common Use Cases

🎯 Best Practices

  • Contrast matters. Light background + dark text OR dark background + light text.
  • Opacity 20-40% for image backgrounds, keep opacity low so text stays readable.
  • Test one page first. Apply to page 1, check the result, then apply to all.
  • Avoid busy images. Simple patterns work better than detailed photos.
  • Choose the right fit. "Cover" for full-page images, "Tile" for repeating patterns.
  • Consider print. Dark backgrounds use lots of ink. For print PDFs, use light colors.
