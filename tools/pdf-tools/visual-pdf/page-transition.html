✨ What are PDF Page Transitions?

PDF page transitions are animation effects that play when you move from one page to the next usually in full-screen or presentation mode. Common styles include Fade (crossfade), Dissolve (particle breakup), Push (slide-in), Wipe (curtain sweep), Split, Box, and Cover. They make PDFs feel like polished presentations or interactive documents.

BigToolz uses pdf-lib to add real /Trans dictionary entries to each PDF page the same standard used by Adobe Acrobat, Foxit, and other professional PDF tools. Everything runs 100% in your browser no uploads, no signup.

🎨 The 8 Transition Styles

🌫️ Fade

Crossfades smoothly from one page to the next. Clean, professional, timeless. Best for corporate presentations and photobooks.

💧 Dissolve

Pixels dissolve like a particle effect. More dynamic than Fade, slightly retro. Great for creative slideshows.

➡️ Push

New page slides in from one edge, pushing the current one out. Direction-aware (left/right/up/down). Perfect for sequential content.

🧹 Wipe

A clean curtain sweeps across to reveal the next page. Direction-aware. Ideal for step-by-step tutorials.

↔️ Split

Two halves slide apart to reveal the next page. Dynamic and cinematic. Great for high-impact slides.

⬜ Box

A growing box reveals the new page from the center outward. Elegant and modern. Best for product reveals.

📄 Cover

The current page slides away, revealing the next one underneath. Direction-aware. Classic and smooth.

✨ Glitter

A sparkling particle effect fades the new page in. Fun and flashy. Perfect for celebration or party slideshows.

📌 Best Practices

  • Duration: 1-2 seconds is the sweet spot. Under 0.5s feels jarring; over 5s feels sluggish.
  • Consistency: Use the same transition on all pages for a polished feel. Mixed transitions feel chaotic.
  • Content-driven: Fade for elegant content, Push for sequential steps, Glitter for celebrations.
  • Direction matters: Push left implies "next", Push right implies "back" use with intent.
  • Test in presentation mode: View in Adobe Acrobat (Ctrl/Cmd + L) to see the actual effect.
