⊞ What are PDF Crop Marks?

Crop marks (also called trim marks or printer's marks) are short lines printed at the corners of a document to indicate where the page should be cut. They're essential when sending a PDF to a commercial printer the marks tell them exactly where the final trim should happen, ensuring accurate edges.

🎨 The 4 Crop Mark Styles

1. Standard (⌐)

Classic crop marks two perpendicular lines at each corner forming an L shape. The universal choice for commercial printing. Lines start at a small offset from the page edge and extend outward.

2. Dashed (- -)

Same layout as Standard but the lines are dashed. Used when you want marks that are visible for alignment but less visually dominant in the printed output (e.g., for in-house printing).

3. Solid (━)

Continuous lines extending beyond the corner. Slightly more prominent than Standard. Good for larger sheets or when the printer needs clear reference lines.

4. Minimal (⌐)

Very short corner ticks just enough to indicate the trim point. Cleanest option, minimal ink usage. Best for modern designs where visual noise should be avoided.

📐 Recommended Settings by Use Case

  • Business cards: Length 18 pt, Width 0.25 pt, Offset 5 pt, Black.
  • Brochures / flyers: Length 20-25 pt, Width 0.5 pt, Offset 5-8 pt, Black.
  • Packaging / labels: Length 25-30 pt, Width 0.5 pt, Offset 6-10 pt, Custom colors (die-cut = red).
  • Books / magazines: Length 20 pt, Width 0.5 pt, Offset 5 pt, Black.
  • In-house printing: Length 15 pt, Width 0.25 pt, Dashed, Offset 3 pt.

💡 Crop Marks vs Bleed vs Safe Area

Crop Marks (Trim Marks)

Short lines outside the page showing where to cut. Standard offset is 5 pt (about 1.75 mm).

Bleed

The extra artwork extending 3-5 mm beyond the trim line, so if cutting is slightly off, there's no white edge. Standard bleed is 3 mm (about 8.5 pt) for most printers, or 5 mm for premium quality.

Safe Area

Where the content (text, logos) should stay usually 3-5 mm inside the trim line. Anything outside the safe area might get cut off or be too close to the edge.
